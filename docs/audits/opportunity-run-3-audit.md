## The principal risk did not materialise

- **#3 Yuwa (A).** Combines philanthropy (`narrative_power`), South Asian diaspora crossover (`cultural_relevance`) and "international CSR campaigns" (`brand_fit`) into a co-branding move with an existing sponsor. **No single finding proposes this**; the intersection is the judgment.

## Strongest reasoning

**#9 Riya — identity ambiguity.** The state's `athletic_authority` rationale says appearances are *"restricted to local youth levels (USAG Level 7) **or** local collegiate club divisions"*, and its evidence hedges twice:

> E1 — *"**A gymnast named** Riya Mehta competed as a Level 7 junior athlete for SGA Gymnastics…"*
> E2 — *"**An athlete named** Riya Mehta represented Veer Narmad South Gujarat University (VNSGU)…"*

**Nothing in State says these may be two people.** The evaluator read the disjunction plus two hedged claims and concluded Nomila may be conflating a US junior with a Gujarat collegiate athlete — then made that the prerequisite to any other work. Cross-evidence reasoning of exactly the kind this layer exists for.

**#8 LeBron — the contradiction, now actionable.** `commercialPosition` reports 0 deals and 0 relationships; `commercial_proof` cites a lifetime Nike contract, Polymarket, Amazon, AT&T, Audemars Piguet and GMC. Run 1 noticed the discrepancy; Run 3 makes it a FIX and names what is missing.

**#11 Lilia — age-appropriate audience move.** Rising momentum (U-14 camps → scoring for the USYNT U-15s in a year) against no personal channels, bounded by a 95 brand-safety score and a "structured family environment". Three inputs, one move.

## The two partials, with the exact unsupported clause

**#7 MAINTAIN / athletic.** The risk is well evidenced — E1 (two-year veteran-minimum contract with Philadelphia, July 2026) and E8 (*"foot joint arthritis and sciatica… missed 17 games"*), both cited by `athletic_authority`. The MAINTAIN discriminator's named risk is satisfied.

> Unsupported: **"Implement strict load management and physical maintenance protocols"**

Two problems. It **prescribes the action**, which the frozen spec forbids in terms — *"You do not… prescribe the action that executes one"* — and nothing in State mentions protocols or load management. Second, it is **outside Nomila's remit**: load management is a performance-staff decision, not a commercial or marketing one. The opportunity would have been sound as *"protect on-court availability, which the current evidence shows is at risk."*

Worth noting the rationale leans slightly against its own source: `athletic_authority`'s prose says *"You remain one of the most authoritative athletic figures globally"* while the opportunity says availability *"is actively threatened."* Not contradictory — authority and availability differ — but the substance comes from cited evidence rather than the rationale.

**#8 FIX / commercial.** The contradiction is fully supported.

> Unsupported: **"costs Nomila the ability to properly run automated conflict checks or schedule renewals"**

State says nothing about conflict checks or renewal scheduling; the capability is assumed. FIX requires a cost paid today, and the real cost — Nomila cannot see relationships it has evidence for — was available and would have sufficed.

Also: this fixes **Nomila's data**, not the athlete's enterprise. `evidence` fits it better than `commercial`.

## Recommendation

**Freeze `opportunity-eval-spec/1.0.0` and proceed to PR3.**

Across three runs the evaluator has been correct where it mattered: it declines when evidence is thin, it does not leak archetypes, it does not default to sponsorship, it reasons across independently owned fields, and 100% of its citations resolve. All five kinds now fire without the taxonomy being touched, which retrospectively identifies the earlier sparsity as an information-boundary problem rather than an evaluator defect.

**Two demonstrated defects, recorded and not blocking:**

1. **Prescription leakage** (#7) — a genuine violation of the spec's own prohibition, 1 of 13.
2. **Invented consequence clauses** (#7, #8) — a supported finding given an unsupported "and this costs us X".

Neither corrupts the map's structure, both are prompt-level, and both become *more* visible at the Objectives layer, which is where a prescription would actually be acted on. Fixing them now costs a spec bump and a rerun to address a 1-in-13 and a 2-in-13 with one observation each — below the 2/2 bar used elsewhere for calling something a pattern rather than variance.

**The one thing I would watch in PR3:** whether Objectives inherits the prescription habit. If it does, that is the moment to correct the boundary in both layers at once.
