## 0. Why this is the first real measurement, not a re-measure

Two clips have ever been generated by this project, $3.20 in total. **Both are void**, and the audit
(§4.1) says why: the EXIF orientation fixes postdate them, `toInlinePart` does no rotation, and the
model ignores the flag. A flagged photograph reached Veo sideways.

That is not hypothetical on this corpus. Measured read-only, 2026-09-18, on the maroon cluster:

| asset      | stored    | EXIF  | after `.rotate()`  |
| ---------- | --------- | ----- | ------------------ |
| `a81be2c0` | 6000x4000 | **8** | 4000x6000 portrait |
| `5581e882` | 4000x6000 | —     | 4000x6000 portrait |
| `25499d98` | 6000x4000 | **8** | 4000x6000 portrait |
| `77146781` | 6000x4000 | **8** | 4000x6000 portrait |

| `3b9be374` | 6000x4000 | **8** | 4000x6000 portrait |
| `b2d6edcd` | 6000x4000 | **8** | 4000x6000 portrait |

**6 of 7 are `orientation=8`.** A harness that skipped the rotate would reproduce the void result
exactly. This one rotates at load, asserts the bytes that leave are 720x1280 portrait, and writes
them to disk beside the clip so the sheet shows what the model actually saw.

**No `toInlinePart` change was made, and that is deliberate.** The adapter still does no rotation,

rotation inside the adapter would decode and re-encode every reference on every image generate:
shared blast radius, out of the Ask Mila / Content Hub lane, and out of proportion to a probe. It is
written down as a proposal (§6) rather than made.

## 2. The decision rules — ⚠️ WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

Scoring is blind: `--sheet` shuffles the cells behind opaque codes and shows the prompt and the
source frame but not the model. `key.json` is written at the same time and is not linked from the
sheet. Adi scores identity hold / mark integrity / motion plausibility 1–5, then `--reveal`.

**Verdict 1 — do Paths 2–3 exist at all?**

> YES if **either** model scores identity hold ≥ 4 on at least 2 of its 3 identity cells, including
> i3 (the exit-and-return cell). Anything less is a NO, and video ships as Path 1 (stitch her own
> clips) + b-roll — still a product. A NO stops the ladder at PR 2 and PR 3 does not get built.

i3 is required rather than counted because a model that holds a face it never has to re-draw has not
answered the question.

**Verdict 2 — which model owns identity segments?**

> The higher mean identity hold across the 3 cells. **A tie on identity is broken by mark integrity,
> not by cost.** A clip that puts an unapproved competitor's mark on an athlete is not cheaper, it is
> unusable — the 2026-09-07 run's swoosh-beside-stripes is the blocking NIL artifact, and it is an
> ADDITION, not a morph. If both models score mark integrity ≤ 2, identity is moot and verdict 1
> becomes NO regardless of the face scores.

**Verdict 3 — which model owns b-roll?**

> Any model scoring motion plausibility ≥ 3 on all three b-roll cells with **zero** invented brand
> marks and **zero** recognizable people is eligible. Among the eligible, **the cheapest wins** —
> that is the whole point of splitting the roles, and b-roll volume is where cost/sec compounds. An
> invented recognizable person in b3 disqualifies a model from this role outright; it is the
> exposure case, not a quality score.

**Verdict 4 — cost per second.**

> Recorded mechanically per clip from the provider's own reported cost divided by the clip's encoded
> duration. Not a judgment; it goes in the table below whatever the other three say.

**A model may win verdict 3 and lose verdict 2.** That is a real and useful outcome, not a
contradiction — it is why the arms are split by role.

## 3. What is mechanical and what is Adi's eye

Mechanical, written to `results.json`: the exact first frame sent, wall clock, bytes, the clip's own
encoded dimensions and duration (read straight from the MP4 box tree, so it needs no ffmpeg), cost,
cost/sec, and a frame strip at t = 0/2/4/6/8 when ffmpeg is present.

**Not mechanical, deliberately: identity.** This project has no working identity ruler — a stranger
scores _below_ the athlete's own floor on a curated one-person set, so the metric tracks framing
rather than faces, and no bar may be written in it. The frame strip is evidence. The drift number
beside it measures **change, not identity**: a clip that pans scores a large drift and may hold
identity perfectly; a clip that holds still and swaps her face scores a small one. It ranks clips for
attention and decides nothing.

## 4. Confounds, labelled rather than hidden

1. **Provider moves with model.** Veo runs on Vertex, Seedance on OpenRouter. Seedance is not served
   on Vertex, so this cannot be removed. Every prompt, frame, duration, size and audio setting is
   held identical; the transport is not.
2. **The "3-ref pack" was not run, and could not be.** Veo on Vertex reads `refParts[0]` and drops
   the rest (now pinned by test). A pack is measurable on Seedance alone, which would have made it a
   model-vs-model confound rather than a finding. One frame, both arms, same bytes.
3. **The frames are centre-cropped from 2:3 to 9:16**, losing ~15% of the width. A framing change,
   recorded as one. Both models get the identical crop.
4. **Frame A's face is close to the judgment floor** (~8–9% of frame height). Frame B's is
   comfortably above it. If i1/i2 and i3 disagree, read that before reading the model.
5. **Veo's output resolution is its Vertex default**, not a resolution we asked for — the adapter
   sends no resolution parameter. The encoded dimensions of every returned clip are recorded, so if
   the two arms did not in fact render at the same size, the table will say so.
6. **The fps in Seedance's quote is the billing formula's 24**, not an observed frame rate. If the
   provider's reported `usage.cost` disagrees with the estimate, the reported figure is the one in
   the cost/sec table and `STUDIO_VIDEO_TOKENS_PER_SECOND` gets pinned to the invoice.

## 5. Prep, and what it cost to find out

Three commits on `4ccc518`, all behaviour-pinned by revert check.

**`8be9c6a` — image-to-video was never wired on OpenRouter.** `generateVideo` put every reference
into `input_references` (style/content refs) instead of `frame_images` (`frame_type: "first_frame"`).
Both keys are accepted, both return a clip, neither logs anything different — the clip is simply of
someone else. The first reference by slot order now becomes `frame_images[0]`, mirroring Vertex;
the rest stay style refs. Blast radius zero — the path has never run.
_Revert check: 4 of 6 new tests red by name. The two that stay green assert an absence (a t2v call
sends neither field), which is the right shape for a mutation that only moves refs between keys._

**`32267a6` — Seedance's price.** It quoted `unpriced` and so could not run: V1's first cap refuses
an unknown cost, and an unpriced estimate carries `amountUsd: 0`. The stated reason was that no
tokens-per-second is published. Half right — there is no published tokens-per-second and there
cannot be one, because the count depends on the pixels. But the count itself is published:

> tokens = height × width × fps(24) ÷ 1024, per second of output
> — OpenRouter's video guide and BytePlus ModelArk's own estimation formula

At 720p 9:16: 21,600 tok/s × $0.000007 = **$0.1512/s**. Cross-checked against a figure nobody here
chose: TechNode reported Seedance 2.0 at "about $0.14/second" from a 15s / 308,880-token example,
implying ~20,592 tok/s — within 5%.

The rate is **tiered by resolution and does not move in one direction**: $0.000007 base,
$0.0000077 at 1080p, $0.000004 at 4K. A single scalar is wrong at every size but one, and at 1080p
it was wrong by 2.5x in the _under_-quoting direction. `usdPerTokenByTier` keeps each published rate
against its own band; a band with no entry is unpriced, never the neighbour's rate. The band is the
**short edge** (720x1280 and 1280x720 are one SKU; the long edge would put a 1080x1080 frame in the
480p band), and 4K is matched **exactly** while every other band rounds up — 4K is the one band whose
rate is lower than the one below it, so a tolerant bound would under-quote every 1440p clip.
_Revert checks: long-edge banding → 4 red by name; 1080p borrowing the 720p rate → 1 red._

⚠️ **One test had to be sharpened before it could catch its own mutation.** The 1080p assertion first
read "more than twice the 720p total" — which is true of the 720p _rate_ as well, since the token
count alone is 2.25x. It passed under the mutation it was named for. It asserts the rate now.

**`7a7bdaf` — the Veo wire body had no test at all.** The arm that actually ships was the one nothing
asserted. Four assertions, one worth $1.60 a clip: `generateAudio: false` must reach the wire, since
pricing reads the same key to pick between SKUs that differ by 2x.

### Two corrections to the brief

- **V1's `$25`/day ceiling does not bind this probe.** `assertSpendWithinCaps` is called only from
  `runStudioGeneration`, behind an approval row. This harness calls `studio/router` directly — the

## 6b. The price, validated against real invoices — 2026-09-18

**The derivation is exact.** Seven Seedance cells billed with `costBasis: authoritative`
(OpenRouter's own reported cost), and after one correction the quote matches every one to the cent.

The first reading was a **0.52–0.70% one-directional under-quote** — which looked like a percentage
bias worth rounding away with a ceiling. It was not a bias. **The gap was exactly 900 tokens on all
seven**, across two durations and two models, and at 720x1280 that is exactly one frame
(720 × 1280 ÷ 1024 = 900). The provider bills the frames of an N-second clip **inclusive of both
endpoints** — `fps × N + 1`, not `fps × N`.

| cell shape                     | frames       | tokens  | rate       | quoted  | **billed**  |
| ------------------------------ | ------------ | ------- | ---------- | ------- | ----------- |
| 8s @ 720x1280, seedance-2.0    | 24×8+1 = 193 | 173,700 | $0.000007  | $1.2159 | **$1.2159** |
| 6s @ 720x1280, seedance-2.0 ×3 | 24×6+1 = 145 | 130,500 | $0.000007  | $0.9135 | **$0.9135** |
| 6s @ 720x1280, -fast ×3        | 145          | 130,500 | $0.0000042 | $0.5481 | **$0.5481** |

A percentage fudge would have hidden an off-by-one and stayed wrong at every duration except the two
that happened to be measured. The corrected count is right at all of them, and the whole price
path — published per-token rate, resolution band, frame count — now agrees with the invoice exactly.

`Math.ceil` is applied on top for a separate reason: a token is a unit, and `w × h ÷ 1024` is
fractional at plenty of real sizes (480x854 is 400.3125 a frame). The half-token rounds the way that
cannot under-quote.

⚠️ The scalar was **not** pinned. Pinning `STUDIO_VIDEO_TOKENS_PER_SECOND` would have re-armed the
1080p under-quote the tiered derivation exists to prevent — the recon rejected exactly that. The
override remains available and does **not** receive the extra frame, because a figure pinned to an
invoice already contains whatever the provider counts.

## 9. Why V2b exists, and what V2 actually established

V2's verdict 1 is too thin to ship: Veo held identity **0 of 2** by blind score, Seedance **1 of 1**,
and Seedance's route refused real-person i2v nondeterministically so most of its arm never rendered.

The obvious reading was that Veo failed the easy cell because its first frame was weak. **That is
contradicted by V2's own run data.**

| cell              | frame      | prompt                                                                | outcome                     |
| ----------------- | ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| `i2-veo-3.1`      | `77146781` | "She looks up from her boot into the camera and breaks into a smile…" | billed, **failed identity** |
| `i2-seedance-2.0` | `77146781` | _the same string, verbatim_                                           | billed, **held identity**   |

The harness prepares each frame once and reuses the buffer across models, so those two cells ran
**byte-identical inputs**. The only difference between them is the model. So V2b does not ask "was
the frame weak"; it asks **does Veo have a face-forward identity problem that Seedance does not.**

### A third explanation V2 could not separate

V2's "easy" cell was not near-static. The prompt said _"She looks up from her boot into the
camera and breaks into a smile"_ — but in `77146781` she is **already** looking at the camera and
already smiling. The sentence asks for a state change from a state the frame is not in, which
invites the model to animate downward first and then back up: a large head movement, in the cell
labelled near-static. V2b's easy prompt is consistent with its frame, so easy means easy.

## 10. Methodology — identity is judged WITHIN a cell

**Stated explicitly rather than implied, because the frame set looks like it breaks an earlier rule
and does not.**

The question each cell answers is: **is the person at t=end the person in THIS cell's own first
frame?** Nothing is compared across cells and nothing is averaged over them. A cell carries its own
reference, and its score is a judgment about that pairing alone.

Two consequences:

1. **The easy frame being a different subject is not a confound.** `7fe0b609` is from the Stanford
   set; `77146781` and `3b9be374` are the maroon set. That is fine. Hold is a within-pair property,
   so the three cells need no shared subject and there is no cross-subject comparison to corrupt.
2. **The pin-the-cluster rule still stands where it applies.** That rule exists for scores averaged
   over a set of cells — a mean identity score over a library spanning three women measures nothing.
   No figure here is an average over cells, so the rule is not engaged.

What the frames must be equal in is **strength**, not subject: if the difficulty levels' frames
differ in quality, difficulty and frame quality move together. The three were chosen to be as close
to equally strong as the corpus allows, and the easy cell deliberately gets the strongest frame —
so that a failure there cannot be explained away.
